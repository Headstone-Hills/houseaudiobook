Command (CPU, int8, beam 5, word timestamps, language en; 4-core x86 container):

    engine/.venv/bin/python scripts/bench_asr.py \
      --models tiny.en small.en medium.en large-v3-turbo \
      --prompt "Um, uh, the- the, I mean, sorry, let me start again."

see engine/fixtures/ground_truth.json). faster-whisper 1.2.1.

## Findings
1. **Without a prompt every model drops stutters** (0 of 2 kept). A disfluent
   `initial_prompt` brings them back for small, medium and turbo (2 of 2). Both
   are small samples: there are only 2 stutters in the fixtures.
2. **small.en + prompt is best here**: 0.0% WER at 0.19x real time. Larger models are
   no more accurate on these fixtures, cost 1.5-2.5x more, and turbo's raw start times
   are worse (440 ms median).
3. **Raw timestamps can't be cut on**: every file's first word starts at 0.00 s (truth
   0.50 s), and errors of 100-400 ms are common. After snapping, small.en + prompt is
   10 / 12 ms at p95, inside D-008's target (median <= 50, p95 <= 150 ms).
4. Whisper emits punctuation-only "words" such as "-" after a stutter; they must be
   ignored for timing and alignment.

## Caveats
- espeak-ng is a robotic voice: tiny/base mishear it ("grapes" -> "braids"). Real
  narration will differ in both directions (more natural, but with breaths, mouth
  noise, room tone). This decision should be re-checked on a real recording.
- 13 sentences and 2-4 events per kind: enough to rank models and expose failure
  modes, not to promise accuracy numbers.
- mlx-whisper was not tested (Apple Silicon only).
